brand.ttf — vendor sign-in marks
================================

This file is NOT nokre's work and is NOT covered by nokre's license.

It exists because nokre's canvas renders paths through exactly one
operation (drawText), so a vendor's sign-in mark has to arrive as a font
glyph the way the Lucide icon set does. It is kept separate from
lucide.ttf and from the text faces on purpose: those carry ISC, OFL, and
Apache upstream licenses, and a trademark glyph inside any of them would
misrepresent that license to everyone who redistributes nokre.

Contents
--------

  U+E900  apple     The Apple logo. A trademark of Apple Inc.
  U+E901  g-blue    The Google "G", blue arc. Part of a trademark of
  U+E902  g-green   Google LLC. One glyph per colored arc, all on one
  U+E903  g-yellow  advance: the renderer overlays the four at a single
  U+E904  g-red     origin and paints each in the color Google mandates.
                    The colors are not in this file — an outline font
                    carries no color — they are applied at draw time
                    from the values in Google's brand guidelines.

The marks in this file are the trademarks of their owners and are used
solely to render the sign-in buttons those owners require, per each
owner's published brand guidelines:

  Apple    developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/
           sign-in-with-apple
  Google   developers.google.com/identity/branding-guidelines

Use of these marks does not imply endorsement of nokre, or of any
application built with it, by their owners. Neither nokre nor its
authors claim any right in them.

The glyph set is closed at the marks listed above. Adding an arbitrary
brand glyph here is a trademark liability rather than a feature, and any
addition must come with the vendor's own artwork and the guideline it is
used under — never a redraw. See tools/make-brand-font.py.

Provenance
----------

Each outline is imported from the owner's own published asset, never
recreated by hand: Apple's guidelines say explicitly not to recreate
their logo, and every other owner says something equivalent.

The Apple outline here is transcribed verbatim from Apple's published
SVG, in its original 814 x 1000 frame; the Google G's four arcs are
transcribed verbatim from the G in Google's published sign-in button
assets, in their original 48 x 48 frame. Both are inlined in
tools/make-brand-font.py — so what is committed can be diffed against
the sources the vendors ship, and nothing about them was drawn by hand.
That script does the mechanical part only: the y-flip into font
coordinates, the scale to cap height, the side bearings, and the
cubic-to-quadratic conversion TrueType requires. Regenerate with:

    python3 tools/make-brand-font.py

It is deterministic — same artwork in, same bytes out — so this file
changes in a commit only when the artwork does.
